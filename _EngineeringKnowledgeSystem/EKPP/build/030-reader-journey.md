# Purpose

This document captures observations about how readers naturally consume engineering knowledge while EKPP is being built.

It does not prescribe visual design.

Instead, it preserves the emerging understanding of the experience EKPP should provide.

These observations may later contribute to AFK if they prove reusable beyond EKPP.

---

# Current Observations

## Observation 001

> **Knowledge should be comfortable to read anywhere.**

---

## Observation 002

Engineering knowledge should feel equally natural to consume:

* during deep work,
* while commuting,
* while waiting for a ride,
* over coffee,
* during short periods of available time.

---

## Observation 003

Reading is the primary interaction.

Navigation exists to support reading, not compete with it.

---

## Observation 004

The interface should reduce cognitive load.

The reader's attention belongs on the knowledge.

---

## Observation 005

Content is the primary interface.

Visual design should quietly support understanding rather than become the focus.

---

## Observation 006

Responsive design is more than a technical requirement.

It is part of the Reader Journey.

The experience should remain comfortable regardless of device or session length.

---

## Observation 007

EKPP should feel like a trusted engineering reference rather than a marketing website or SaaS product.

---

# Open Questions

This document intentionally remains exploratory.

Questions currently under investigation include:

* What visual language best supports engineering knowledge?
* How should long-form reading differ between desktop and mobile?
* Should Reader Journey become part of AFK?
* Is Reader Journey unique to EKPP or reusable across other engineering knowledge systems?

---

# Current Assessment

The Reader Journey has emerged naturally during the implementation of EKPP.

More observations are required before determining whether this body of knowledge should evolve into a reusable AFK concept.
