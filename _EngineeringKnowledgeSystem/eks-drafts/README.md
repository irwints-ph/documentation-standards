# Purpose

The **Engineering Knowledge System (EKS)** is an evolving collection of engineering philosophies, methodologies, patterns, and practices developed through **AI-assisted collaboration**.

Unlike project documentation, which explains a single implementation, EKS seeks to understand **how engineering knowledge itself is created, validated, preserved, transferred, and continuously improved**.

Its purpose is not merely to document software.

Its purpose is to improve **how engineers learn.**

---

# Vision

Build a future where engineering knowledge no longer depends on individual memory.

Instead, knowledge becomes a reusable organizational asset that grows through every project, every discovery, and every engineering challenge.

---

# Core Philosophy

The Engineering Knowledge System is founded on a simple belief:

> **Engineering knowledge should outlive the engineers who created it.**

Knowledge should not disappear because:

* someone changed teams,
* someone retired,
* documentation was never written,
* or everyone simply forgot.

Instead, engineering knowledge should become part of an organization's collective capability.

# AI-Assisted Collaboration

Artificial Intelligence is not treated as a replacement for engineering expertise.

Instead, EKS promotes **AI-assisted collaboration**, where engineers and AI work together throughout the engineering lifecycle.

Typical collaboration includes:

* Learning unfamiliar systems
* Reverse engineering
* Architecture discovery
* Documentation
* Validation
* Pattern recognition
* Knowledge organization
* Modernization planning
* Knowledge preservation

AI accelerates learning.

Engineers remain responsible for engineering judgment.

---

# Engineering Learning

Learning is considered the primary deliverable.

Every engineering activity has the potential to produce reusable knowledge.

Examples include:

* solving production incidents,
* documenting legacy systems,
* performing architecture reviews,
* modernizing existing software,
* onboarding new engineers,
* validating engineering standards.

Engineering work should continuously improve both software **and** engineering capability.

# Assisted Flow of Knowledge (AFK)

One methodology currently emerging from the Engineering Knowledge System is:

## AFK — Assisted Flow of Knowledge

AFK explores how engineering knowledge moves efficiently between:

* Engineers
* AI assistants
* Software systems
* Documentation
* Projects
* Organizations

Rather than depending on conversation history or individual memory, AFK promotes preserving engineering context through structured artifacts that enable work to resume quickly and consistently.

Current AFK research includes:

* AI-assisted collaboration
* AI-assisted learning
* Discovery workflows
* Knowledge packages
* Session continuity
* Engineering mindset
* Canon Events
* Organizational learning

AFK remains experimental and is being validated through real engineering projects.
