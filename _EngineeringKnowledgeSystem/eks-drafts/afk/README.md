# Purpose

**Assisted Flow of Knowledge (AFK)** is an emerging methodology for **engineering discovery through human and AI collaboration**.

Rather than using AI simply to generate answers or produce code, AFK helps engineers **discover**, **understand**, **learn**, **preserve**, and **transfer** engineering knowledge.

AFK does not assume the answers already exist.

It provides a disciplined way to discover them together.

The objective is not faster code generation.

The objective is **better engineers through better understanding.**

---

# Vision

Create an engineering workflow where every task contributes to long-term organizational knowledge rather than temporary output.

Every investigation...

Every bug...

Every architecture review...

Every modernization effort...

Every engineering conversation...

becomes an opportunity to improve both the engineer and the organization.

---

# Core Philosophy

AFK begins with a simple observation.

> **Engineering is not the act of writing code.**
>
> **Engineering is the act of understanding systems.**

Code is usually the final output.

Understanding is the real product.

---

# Discovery Mindset

Engineering rarely begins with certainty.

It begins with curiosity.

AFK embraces the unknown.

Rather than assuming understanding, AFK encourages engineers to investigate, validate, document, and learn.

Instead of asking:

> "Who already knows the answer?"

AFK asks:

> **"How can we discover the answer together?"**

Knowledge naturally flows through engineering work.

```text

↓

↓

Understanding

↓

Documentation

↓

↓

Engineering Knowledge
```

AFK encourages engineers to deliberately preserve that flow instead of allowing valuable knowledge to disappear once the task is complete.

# AI-Assisted Collaboration

AFK treats AI as an **engineering companion**, not an engineering replacement.

The engineer contributes:

* context
* experience
* engineering judgment
* validation
* decision making
* curiosity

The AI companion contributes:

* organization
* exploration
* explanation
* synthesis
* documentation
* continuity
* pattern recognition

The engineer remains responsible for engineering judgment.

The AI accelerates engineering discovery.

Neither replaces the other.

Together they accelerate engineering learning.

# Discovery Before Judgment

AFK encourages understanding before evaluation.

Rather than immediately deciding whether something is good or bad, right or wrong, AFK first asks:

* What is it?
* Why does it exist?
* What problem was it trying to solve?
* What can it teach us?

Only after understanding should engineering judgment be applied.

AFK does not begin with conclusions.

AFK begins with discovery.

AFK encourages different engineering questions.

Instead of:

> "How do I fix this?"

Ask:

> "What is this teaching me about the system?"

Instead of:

> "What code should I write?"

Ask:

> "What knowledge should we preserve?"

Instead of:

> "Who caused the problem?"

Ask:

> "How can we improve the system so this becomes less likely?"

Instead of:

> "Is this a good idea?"

> **"Let's AFK it and discover what it is."**

AFK shifts attention from assigning blame toward improving understanding.

# Operational Continuity

Engineering understanding should survive beyond a single conversation.

AFK introduces the concept of **Context Bootstrap Documents**.

The primary artifact is:

**WWAN — Where We Are Now**

WWAN represents the **current operational memory** of a project.

It allows engineers and AI companions to resume work quickly without rebuilding context from previous conversations.

WWAN is intentionally maintained as a living document.

It is **not** a historical log.

History explains how the project arrived here.

WWAN explains where the project is today.

Supporting AFK artifacts include:

* WWAN
* Discovery Plans
* Architecture Findings
* Component Documentation
* Engineering Standards
* Knowledge Packages

Together they provide engineering continuity across sessions, contributors, and time.
