# Purpose

Every engineering collaboration begins somewhere.

AFK intentionally defines how an engineering session starts.

The objective is not to immediately solve a problem.

The objective is to establish enough shared understanding so meaningful discovery can begin.

This document describes how both human collaborators and AI collaborators enter an AFK session.

---

# Philosophy

AFK does not assume that every collaborator already understands AFK.

Every collaboration begins by building shared context.

Rather than assuming knowledge...

AFK acquires it.

---

# Guiding Principle

> **Don't wish for what you can't influence. Design for what you can.**

AFK cannot assume every AI collaborator already understands AFK.

Instead, AFK projects are designed to teach collaborators how they work.

Every new collaboration becomes an opportunity to establish context.

---

# The Human Beginning

People rarely begin engineering conversations with architecture.

They begin with a story.

Examples include:

> "I have an idea..."

> "Something has been bothering me..."

> "I wish this system could..."

> "Can I tell you something?"

Humans naturally communicate through stories.

AFK embraces this.

---

# The First Question

Rather than immediately asking for requirements, AFK begins by understanding the situation.

A typical opening may simply be:

> **Tell me the story.**

The objective is understanding before solution.

# AI Bootstrap

An AI collaborator should never assume project knowledge.

Instead, it should establish context before contributing.

A typical bootstrap process may include:

1. Read the project's WWAN.
2. Read AFK Purpose.
3. Read AFK Culture.
4. Read the project's current WWAN.
5. Read any current Discovery registry.
6. Confirm current operational context.

Only then should engineering discovery begin.

---

# Why Bootstrap?

Every project has:

* different objectives,
* different terminology,
* different architecture,
* different history,
* different active wishes.

Bootstrapping reduces unnecessary assumptions and allows collaborators to begin with shared understanding.

---

# Invitation to Collaborate

An AFK session intentionally begins with invitation.

Examples include:

> Let's AFK this.

> Can we AFK this together?

> I'd like to start an AFK session.

The invitation signals that the objective is collaborative discovery rather than immediate solution generation.

---

# The Role of Curiosity

AFK begins with curiosity.

Not certainty.

Every session starts from the assumption that there is something valuable still waiting to be discovered.

Questions are encouraged.

Assumptions are postponed.

Understanding comes first.

---

# Bootstrap Success

A successful bootstrap does not end with a solution.

It ends when everyone involved shares enough understanding to begin meaningful discovery.

Only then does AFK move forward.

---

# Relationship to AFK

Session Bootstrap is intentionally lightweight.

It does not replace Discovery.

It prepares collaborators for Discovery.

Everything that follows depends upon the quality of this first step.

---

# Closing Thought

Every engineering journey begins somewhere.

AFK chooses to begin with understanding.

Not requirements.

Not architecture.

Not implementation.

Simply...

> **Tell me the story.**

Everything else can be discovered together.
