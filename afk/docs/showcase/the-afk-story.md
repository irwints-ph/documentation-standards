# The AFK Story

People often ask where ideas come from.

Most expect a moment of inspiration.

A whiteboard.

A grand design.

A carefully planned methodology.

The truth is much simpler.

It started because I was tired.

Not tired of engineering.

Tired of documenting the same things over and over.

Every new project seemed to begin with rebuilding the same understanding.

Every pause required rediscovering the same context.

Every handover depended on memory more than evidence.

So I asked a simple question:

> *"How do I make documentation less painful?"*

The answer wasn't another template.

It was another question.

---

Months later another problem appeared.

Projects paused.

Life happened.

Returning meant reconstructing everything from scattered notes and fading memory.

So another question emerged.

> *"How do I restart projects after months?"*

---

Then AI arrived.

Like many engineers, I experimented.

At first it answered questions.

Then it generated code.

Then it became useful enough that I found myself explaining the same context repeatedly.

Once again another question appeared.

> *"How do I collaborate with AI without repeating myself?"*

---

The deeper we explored, the clearer something became.

The problem was never documentation.

The problem was preserving understanding.

Documentation records information.

Understanding explains why the information matters.

Somewhere during countless conversations, architecture reviews, engineering experiments, and documentation sessions, the question finally changed.

> *"How do I preserve engineering state?"*

That question became the turning point.

---

Without intending to, a pattern emerged.

Engineering Documentation System.

Engineering Replay.

Where We Are Now.

Capability Matrix.

Evidence Matrix.

Resume Engineering.

Knowledge Packages.

Project Bootstrap.

Session Reactivation.

Each solved a different problem.

Together they solved a larger one.

They formed something neither planned.

---

# Assisted Flow of Knowledge

AFK was never designed in a single sitting.

It emerged one engineering problem at a time.

Every improvement reduced friction.

Every document reduced reconstruction.

Every replay shortened onboarding.

Every standard preserved a little more engineering understanding.

Eventually the individual solutions became a system.

Not because someone decided to invent a methodology.

Because engineering naturally demanded one.

---

# The Philosophy

I believe engineering should become easier for the next person.

Whether that next person is:

* another engineer,
* my future self,
* or an AI collaborator.

Knowledge should survive its creator.

Engineering understanding should not disappear when a conversation ends.

Good systems should help future engineers continue rather than begin again.

# Looking Forward

AFK is still evolving.

Every project validates it.

Every experiment refines it.

Every conversation reveals another missing piece.

Perhaps one day it becomes an accepted engineering methodology.

Perhaps it becomes part of a doctoral dissertation.

Or perhaps it simply helps one engineer pick up where another left off.

Either outcome would make the journey worthwhile.
