# Purpose

Define Capability Calibration as one of AFK's foundational concepts.

The objective is not to evaluate people, but to improve the accuracy of how they understand themselves through structured collaboration and evidence.

---

# Background

People rarely have a perfectly accurate understanding of their own capabilities.

Some underestimate what they can do.

Others overestimate what they know.

Many simply struggle to articulate experiences they have already lived.

The result is not a capability problem.

It is a perception problem.

---

# Core Principle

AFK does not attempt to increase capability.

AFK first attempts to improve the accuracy of **perceived capability**.

Only after perception becomes more accurate can meaningful growth occur.

---

# Engineering Inspiration

Engineering systems are continuously calibrated.

Measurements drift.

Processes evolve.

Evidence accumulates.

Calibration restores confidence that decisions are based on reality rather than assumption.

AFK applies the same principle to human knowledge.

---

# Capability Calibration

```text id="n5pc7g"
Experience

↓

Evidence

↓

Reflection

↓

Structured Collaboration

↓

Capability Calibration

↓

More Accurate Self-Understanding
```

The objective is not to produce praise or criticism.

The objective is to reduce the gap between perceived capability and demonstrated capability.

---

# Sources of Calibration

Capability calibration may come from multiple sources:

* personal reflection,
* documented evidence,
* engineering artifacts,
* mentors,
* peers,
* AI collaboration.

AI is one collaborator among many.

It is not the objective.

---

# Evidence Before Opinion

AFK encourages capability discussions to begin with observable evidence rather than assumptions.

Examples include:

* projects,
* architecture decisions,
* documentation,
* leadership experiences,
* technical outcomes,
* lessons learned.

Evidence creates a more objective foundation for understanding capability.

---

# Outcomes

Capability Calibration may produce several outcomes.

## Underestimated Capability

The individual discovers strengths they had not previously recognized.

## Overestimated Capability

The individual identifies areas requiring additional growth.

## Missing Capability

Evidence reveals genuine opportunities for learning.

Each outcome is considered valuable because it improves understanding rather than reinforcing assumptions.

---

# Relationship to AFK

Many AFK systems exist primarily to support Capability Calibration.

Examples include:

* Engineering Documentation System
* Replay Documents
* Where We Are Now
* Resume Engineering
* Engineering Capability Matrix
* Capability Evidence Matrix

These systems preserve evidence so that future reflection becomes easier and more accurate.

---

# Future Self

AFK places significant emphasis on helping Present You communicate with Future You.

Capability Calibration reduces the loss of context that naturally occurs over time.

Rather than relying on memory alone, future decisions can be supported by documented evidence.

---

# Beyond Engineering

Although inspired by engineering practices, Capability Calibration is not limited to engineering.

The same principles may support:

* education,
* careers,
* financial planning,
* leadership,
* continuous learning,
* personal development.

Engineering provides the methodology.

The outcome is broader self-understanding.

---

# Mission Control Observation

People rarely need someone to tell them who they are.

More often, they need help recognizing patterns that already exist.

Capability Calibration uses evidence and structured collaboration to reduce the distance between actual capability and perceived capability.

AFK therefore becomes less about generating documents and more about helping individuals develop a more accurate understanding of themselves over time.
