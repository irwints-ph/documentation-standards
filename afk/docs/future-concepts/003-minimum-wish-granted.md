# Purpose

Preserve an emerging idea discovered during the Engineering Knowledge Publishing Portal (EKPP) architecture discussion.

The concept is intentionally recorded without attempting to standardize it.

Its purpose is preservation, not adoption.

---

# Status

This concept is **experimental**.

It has not been validated through engineering practice and is **not currently part of AFK**.

Whether it becomes part of the methodology remains to be discovered.

---

# Discovery Context

During EKPP planning, a discussion emerged around keeping the initial implementation intentionally small.

Traditional software development often asks:

> "What is the Minimum Viable Product (MVP)?"

The conversation naturally shifted toward a different question.

> **"What is the smallest meaningful wish we can genuinely grant?"**

This became the beginning of the Minimum Wish Granted concept.

---

# The Central Idea

Instead of measuring progress by the amount of software delivered, MWG proposes measuring progress by the number of meaningful wishes successfully granted.

The objective is not building the smallest product.

The objective is granting the smallest meaningful outcome for someone.

---

# Example

Instead of asking:

> What is the smallest documentation website we can build?

The discussion became:

> I wish I could see the initial output of EKPP.

Everything else became secondary.

Search.

Automation.

Versioning.

Comments.

CloudFront.

These remained valid wishes, but they no longer blocked delivering the first meaningful outcome.

---

# Relationship to Wish Engineering

MWG naturally emerged from the Wish Engineering discussion.

Wish Engineering asks:

> **"Whose wish are we trying to grant?"**

Minimum Wish Granted asks:

> **"What is the smallest meaningful wish we can grant today?"**

The concepts appear related, although neither has been validated.

---

# Emerging Observations

Several ideas surfaced during discussion.

None have been validated.

---

## 🌱 One Wish at a Time

Rather than attempting to satisfy every wish immediately, engineering effort may become more focused by selecting a single meaningful wish.

Grant it well.

Learn from it.

Then discover the next wish.

---

## 🔍 Discovery Before Expansion

Granting one wish creates operational experience.

Operational experience often reveals wishes that could never have been predicted during planning.

This suggests that granting one real wish may be more valuable than designing for many hypothetical ones.

---

## 📥 Queued Wishes

The discussion also introduced the idea that not every wish should immediately become active work.

Some wishes may remain queued until sufficient understanding exists.

A queued wish is not rejected.

It is simply waiting for its appropriate time.

---

# Questions Worth Exploring

Several questions remain unanswered.

* Does focusing on one meaningful wish improve engineering outcomes?
* Does MWG reduce unnecessary implementation?
* Does it encourage earlier operational feedback?
* Does it naturally produce better architecture?
* Is MWG fundamentally different from MVP, or simply another way of describing incremental delivery?

These questions remain open.

---

# Current Decision

Minimum Wish Granted is intentionally **not** part of AFK today.

It is preserved as a Future Concept while additional engineering experience determines whether it provides lasting value.

---

# Closing Thought

Perhaps successful engineering is not measured by how much software is delivered.

Perhaps it is measured by how many meaningful wishes are genuinely granted.

Or perhaps not.

We do not know yet.

**Let's AFK it together.**
