# Discovery Context

During EKPP discovery, a simple observation emerged.

Software projects often begin by discussing **features**, **requirements**, or **solutions**.

However, behind every feature request is usually something much more human.

Someone wishes the system could do something.

Rather than immediately asking:

> "How should we build this?"

AFK began asking:

> **"What is the wish?"**

This subtle change shifted the discussion away from implementation and toward intent.

---

# The Central Idea

A **Wish** represents the desired human outcome before implementation is discussed.

It deliberately avoids technical solutions.

Instead of writing:

> Add an online Markdown editor.

The underlying wish might be:

> I wish updating documentation was easier.

Multiple implementations may satisfy the same wish.

Understanding the wish first allows architecture to emerge naturally rather than prematurely.

---

# Why "Wish"?

The word "wish" intentionally feels human.

Requirements often describe solutions.

Wishes describe intent.

By starting with wishes, engineers remain focused on solving the real problem rather than the first implementation that comes to mind.

## 🧞 AI Assistant Genie

A playful metaphor emerged during discussion.

Rather than acting as an answer generator, the AI behaves like an Assistant Genie.

Instead of immediately granting wishes, the Genie helps engineers understand them.

Possible questions include:

* What is the real wish?
* Does another wish already exist?
* Is this wish actually solving a different problem?
* Have similar wishes appeared before?

The Genie assists discovery rather than replacing engineering judgment.

---

## ☕ Wish Review

Another idea emerged while discussing conflicting wishes.

Instead of immediately implementing every wish, the Genie might ask:

> "Would you like to talk before I grant either one?"

Humorously followed by:

> "I have reserved a coffee shop for both of you."

Behind the joke is a serious engineering principle.

Conflicting intentions should be discussed before architecture begins.

The conversation happens before the code.

# Validation Needed

Before Wish Engineering could become part of AFK, questions remain.

* Does beginning with wishes consistently improve engineering decisions?
* Does it reduce premature solution design?
* Does it improve communication between engineers?
* Can it scale beyond small teams?
* Does it remain practical during large projects?

These questions remain unanswered.

# Current Decision

Wish Engineering is intentionally **not** part of AFK today.

It is preserved as a Future Concept so it can evolve naturally through experience rather than being standardized prematurely.
